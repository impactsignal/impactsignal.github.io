Google and Gates Foundation Put $100 Million Behind AI Tools for 200 Million Smallholder Farmers

On September 18, 2026, Google and the Gates Foundation unveiled an expanded initiative bringing AI tools to smallholder farmers across Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, committing $100 million in combined funding plus dedicated research and engineering support. The multi-year roadmap scales the program from an initial reach of 50 million farmers to a target of 200 million, covering climate-risk forecasting, planting-cycle guidance, pest management, and resource allocation. The systems are built to run in low-connectivity areas and across diverse languages, aimed at smallholder operations that make up roughly 35% of global food production — more than 500 million farms — but typically lack access to satellite data, financial services, and public agricultural support. Why it matters: This is a concrete scale commitment, not a pilot announcement: a stated jump from 50 million to 200 million farmers reached, with a dollar figure and named tools attached rather than a vague "AI for agriculture" pledge. Google and the Gates Foundation say the roadmap runs over multiple years as the program scales toward its 200-million target.

Sources: Gates Foundation, Google.org, HyperAI

Microsoft's Open-Source Damage-Mapping AI Gave Catholic Relief Services an Overnight Assessment After Venezuela's Earthquake

Microsoft published a detailed technical account on September 22, 2026, of how its open-source HASTE disaster-assessment system produced a building-damage map for Venezuela's June 2026 earthquake by the morning after the quake, giving Catholic Relief Services and local partners an earlier basis for deciding which communities needed help first. Running AI-based assessment across five post-disaster satellite scenes of the La Guaira/Vargas coast — Catia La Mar, La Guaira, and Caraballeda — Microsoft's AI for Good Research Lab assessed 72,162 distinct buildings across roughly 210 square kilometers, flagging 8,410 (about 12%) as damaged, with confidence scores letting responders cross-check the AI read against other assessments. Why it matters: The number that matters for other humanitarian technologists is the turnaround: a full damage assessment overnight rather than the days a manual survey would take, using an open-source tool a partner organization — not Microsoft itself — used to decide where to send people and supplies first. The system is designed so analysts without machine-learning training can adapt it to a new disaster.

Sources: Windows Forum, Microsoft AI for Good

Google Commits 100,000 Free AI-Skills Scholarships Through an ITU Partnership Unveiled at the UN General Assembly

At Digital@UNGA 2026, Google and the International Telecommunication Union announced the AI Skills Coalition on September 22, 2026, offering 100,000 free certificate scholarships in AI, data, IT, and design across more than 80 countries. The scholarships are delivered through national governments and the ITU/UNICEF Giga network — the initiative that connects schools to the internet — and are aimed at people without a technical background: students, teachers, job seekers, small-business owners, and public servants. Why it matters: Routing the training through Giga's existing government partnerships, rather than general open enrollment, is the detail that determines whether the 100,000 scholarships reach people who would otherwise have no path to AI-skills training or stay concentrated among people who already have it. Google and the ITU say delivery runs through national governments as the coalition rolls out across its 80-plus countries.

Sources: Google (Grow with Google), PM News Nigeria

Colorado Springs Put an AI Agent on Non-Emergency Police and Fire Calls, and It Handled Half of Them Without a Human

The Colorado Springs Public Safety Communications Center formally launched "Prepared by Axon" on September 22, 2026, an AI system that transcribes calls in real time, translates conversations in more than 70 languages, summarizes information for dispatchers, and answers non-emergency police and fire calls. The department tested it for four days — 54 total hours — before launch; in that window, the AI resolved 49% of non-emergency calls without rerouting them to a human call-taker. Officials were explicit that the system is not being used for actual 911 emergency calls. Why it matters: The 49%-of-54-hours figure is a real before/after number from the department's own pilot, not a vendor claim, and the real-time 70-language translation addresses a concrete access gap for non-English-speaking callers to public safety services. Colorado Springs officials say the system remains restricted to non-emergency lines only.

Sources: KKTV, KRDO, Colorado Springs Police Department

New York City's Ban on AI in Classrooms Through 8th Grade Is Drawing a New Fight Over Whether It Widens the Digital Divide

City & State New York published new reporting on September 21, 2026, examining whether NYC's generative-AI classroom ban — which covers nearly 600,000 public-school students from 2K through 8th grade — will widen the city's existing digital divide. The piece surfaces a tension raised by education advocates: for students to build real AI fluency they need to use the technology, and under-resourced schools and families are the ones least likely to get supervised, high-quality exposure to it outside the ban's narrow, closely-monitored high-school pilot programs. The city's policy does keep twice-yearly AI-literacy lessons — covering bias, ethics, and critical thinking — for all high schoolers. Why it matters: This is the accountability follow-up to a headline-grabbing policy: a specific, named equity risk — who gets supervised AI exposure and who doesn't — attached to a law affecting two-thirds of the country's largest school district. The ban's high-school pilot programs remain the only sanctioned in-school exposure point as the policy stands.

Sources: City & State New York, Route Fifty
