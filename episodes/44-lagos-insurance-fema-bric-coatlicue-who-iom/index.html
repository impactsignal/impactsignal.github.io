🔥 Top Stories

Lagos Goes Live with Satellite-Triggered Parametric Flood Insurance

Lagos State deployed automatic flood insurance covering up to 4 million vulnerable residents, with payouts up to $7.5M triggered when ICEYE radar satellites detect flood depths crossing 50cm across 7 zones — no claims filing required. Partners: AXA Climate, AXA Mansard Nigeria, Swiss Re (reinsurer), JBA Risk Management. InsuResilience Solutions Fund (UNDP/IDF/BMZ) covered 90% of first-year premiums. Lagos faces $40B in cumulative climate losses by 2050. Combined with the AI coastal water monitoring system announced earlier this week, Lagos now has a complete climate resilience stack: detection + automatic recovery finance. Nigerian federal government evaluating national scale-up.

FEMA BRIC Restored: $1 Billion for Disaster Mitigation

FEMA will reopen the Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities (BRIC) program after a coalition of 22 states won a court order from U.S. District Judge Richard Stearns. Program was unilaterally canceled in April 2025, freezing $3.6B in previously approved projects. Now restoring: $757M national competition, $112M state/territorial allocations, $50M tribal nations set-aside, $81M building code adoption. Applications close July 23. Every $1 in disaster prep saves $13 in economic impact (2024 study). Tribal set-aside is relevant for Native nations adopting culturally-grounded AI flood tools (see UT Austin AI² project).

Mexico's Coatlicue Supercomputer: 314K Trillion Ops/Sec for Climate Forecasting

President Claudia Sheinbaum committed 6 billion pesos (~$340M) to Coatlicue, named after the Aztec goddess. 100× more powerful than Mexico's current top system; 7× more powerful than Brazil's Pegaso. Primary purpose: extreme weather forecasting and climate disaster early warning. Creates regional leadership potential for Central America (Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Belize) — countries facing similar climate exposure with far less computational capacity. Deployment timeline and technology partner TBD.

IOM Joins WHO Global Digital Health Certification Network

WHO announced IOM has joined GDHCN as its first international organization member (March 26). IOM's mandate: extend portable health verification to displacement settings with limited connectivity. Built on COVID-era travel health infrastructure; now evolving into privacy-preserving portable vaccination/medical records for displaced populations. Aligned with UNHCR Digital Gateway (2026 rollout) and WFP BRaVe biometric expansion.
