Philippines' Education Department Shows Project SABAY, a 15-Minute AI Screen for Learning and Speech Difficulties in Grade 2

The Philippine Department of Education (DepEd) presented Project SABAY, short for Screening using AI-Based Assistance for Young Children, on Wednesday, November 18, BusinessWorld and the Philippine News Agency reported the same day. It is a teacher-assisted application in which a Grade 2 learner works through story-driven, game-like speech tasks: naming pictures, word dictation, nonsense-word dictation and speaking freely. Education Secretary Sonny Angara said a teacher will be able to check "in about 15 minutes" whether a child may need extra help with learning or speech; the Multi-Factored Assessment Tool it is compared with takes about two hours per learner, according to the department. The tool was built in-house by DepEd's Bureau of Learning Delivery, Student Inclusion Division, and the Education Center for AI Research. So far DepEd has pilot tested it in Metro Manila and expanded data collection across the Visayas and Mindanao, the Philippine News Agency reported. Why it matters: The case DepEd makes is a shortage of the people who can give a diagnosis. BusinessWorld cites the Second Congressional Commission on Education: one developmental pediatrician covers 3,096 learners in Luzon, 8,718 in the Visayas and 11,127 in Mindanao, and as of June, 109 specialists across 156 clinics served about five million learners with disabilities, with waiting lists that DepEd says can stretch to a year or more. A 15-minute first screen moves that bottleneck without removing it, because a flagged child still needs one of those specialists. No accuracy figures and no independent evaluation were cited in the three reports read, and a third outlet, ASTIG, noted the announcement did not say how many learners have been screened, how many schools use the tool, what it costs or how the children's speech recordings are stored. DepEd says data collection outside Metro Manila continues so the tool can handle more of the country's languages; no date for nationwide use has been given.

Sources: BusinessWorld, Philippine News Agency (via The Chronicle), ASTIG

NASCIO's First State AI Officer Survey: At Least 46 US States Have Someone Directing AI, and 56 Percent of Those Surveyed Have No AI Budget

The National Association of State Chief Information Officers (NASCIO) published its first State AI Officer Survey on Tuesday, November 17, StateScoop and Government Technology reported the same day. It draws on the officials leading AI work in 33 states and territories. When NASCIO began tracking in 2023, fewer than 20 percent of states had a chief AI officer or similar role; now at least 46 states have identified at least one person tasked with directing AI, and 25 have someone with AI in their title. Seventy-two percent of respondents said their remit covers all executive branch agencies, but only 44 percent can independently set enterprise AI policies and standards, another 16 percent share that power, and just over half can enforce compliance. Fifty-six percent have no defined or dedicated AI budget, 75 percent named insufficient staffing among their biggest challenges, and only 42 percent have AI as their primary responsibility, per Government Technology. Why it matters: State agencies run the benefits, licensing, health and emergency systems that people in the United States deal with directly, so who can say yes or no to an AI use inside a state government is a question about those services. On paper the rules exist: 97 percent of respondents said their state has AI policies or guidance, and 83 percent have a process for agencies to submit proposed AI uses for review. The survey measures the distance between that paper and the means to apply it, in figures from the officials themselves, most of whose positions were created by a chief information officer's directive and not by legislation or executive order. Ninety percent expect agentic AI, systems that complete tasks and take actions with less human involvement, to take up significantly more of their time over the next three years. NASCIO's recommendation, quoted by StateScoop, is that these officials "would benefit from having full authority to establish and enforce enterprise AI standards"; the survey does not record any state committing to that.

Sources: StateScoop, Government Technology

Malaysia Tests Cell Broadcast Disaster Alerts on November 19, With Nationwide Operation Expected by the End of 2026

Malaysia's communications regulator, the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC), told The Star in a report published Wednesday, November 18, that a national early warning system for natural disasters is expected to go live nationwide by the end of 2026. A test is scheduled for Thursday, November 19, described by MCMC as technical readiness testing of the whole chain, from the start of an alert to its arrival on a handset; The Star said the pilot was expected to focus on high-risk flood areas including Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, Kelantan and Pahang. This is not an AI story. The change is the delivery channel: MCMC and the National Disaster Management Agency (Nadma) are building the system on cell broadcast, which sends an alert to every phone in an affected area without needing internet, SMS or the recipients' phone numbers. Deputy Prime Minister Ahmad Zahid Hamidi has said it is meant eventually to replace the current SMS-based system, and The Star reported it is also being developed to serve people with visual or hearing disabilities. Why it matters: A warning that depends on a subscriber list or a data connection misses the people who are not on the list or not online, and Malaysia is moving its national alerts onto a channel that addresses a place, not a set of numbers. Nadma coordinates the warning protocols and the relevant agencies decide when an alert goes out, drawing on the Meteorological Department, the Irrigation and Drainage Department and the National Minerals and Geoscience Department. Universiti Malaya meteorologist Azizan Abu Samah told The Star the system suits urban flash floods but that there is "a very narrow time window" because the water can recede quickly, and that a warning must say whether it is a flash flood or major flooding. The report gave no figures for how many phones or what share of the country the test will reach, and the test had not taken place at the time of writing.

Sources: The Star (Malaysia), Asia News Network

Chile Signs a Memorandum With OpenAI to Train Civil Servants and Bring AI Into State Services: First Phase Free, Later Projects Paid

Chile's Minister of Science, Technology, Knowledge and Innovation, Ximena Lincolao, signed a memorandum of understanding with OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman in San Francisco, Chilean outlets Meganoticias and El Mostrador reported on November 16 and 17. The initiative, OpenAI para Chile, sits under the company's OpenAI for Countries program and has three lines of work: bringing AI tools into the state's administrative processes, a practical training program for public employees adapted to each worker's role (using the tools, checking their outputs and protecting sensitive information), and support for Chilean startups. The ministry said the first phase costs the state nothing and is open to any public body that wants to take part. In a second phase, public services that decide to pursue specific projects will have to pay for their development, El Mostrador reported; the announcement does not say how much or which projects. Lincolao said Chile is the first Latin American country to set up this kind of direct collaboration with the company, a claim that was not checked independently. Why it matters: The structure is now on the record for a national government: training and initial adoption arrive free through a vendor's country program, and the costs begin when an agency wants something specific built, at prices nobody has published. That arrangement decides which company's tools a country's civil servants learn first, before any tender. The memorandum lists conditions, including compliance with existing law, personal data protection, information security and human oversight, and the ministry said controls and evaluations will be identified before each use case is implemented or expanded. It is the second AI agreement the minister announced in a week, after one with the startup ElevenLabs, per El Mostrador. A joint working committee of the ministry and OpenAI is expected to meet monthly in the initial phase, Meganoticias reported; no project list or start date was reported.

Sources: El Mostrador, Meganoticias
