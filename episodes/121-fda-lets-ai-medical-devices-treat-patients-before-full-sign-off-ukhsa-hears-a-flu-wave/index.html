#121: Impact Signals #121 — FDA Lets AI Medical Devices Treat Patients Before Full Sign-Off, UKHSA Hears a Flu Wave Coming From Sleep-App Coughs, Jonesboro PD Names Its AI Dispatcher After the Woman Whose Job It Now Does

FDA's TEMPO Pilot Lets AI Medical Devices Reach Patients Before Full Authorization

The FDA's new TEMPO pilot admits a first cohort of four companies — Cadence, Limbic, Dexcom, and SonderMind — whose generative-AI-enabled medical devices can reach patients under a provisional pathway, without full marketing authorization, as long as the companies feed real-world performance data back to regulators. The pilot is explicitly built to expand the pool of technologies eligible for the Medicare ACCESS model, the federal government's program paying providers to use tools that help beneficiaries manage chronic conditions between visits. Why it matters: For an agency that has been the industry's most conservative gatekeeper, a narrow, monitored pathway is a signal that generative AI in clinical care has moved from research question to regulatory infrastructure question. The Medicare ACCESS tie-in makes the near-term test case chronic-disease management for an older, sicker population. The FDA is taking public comment on the approach through October 19, 2026.

Sources: STAT, FDA

UKHSA Shows a Sleep App's Cough Data Predicts Flu a Week Early

UKHSA and the Swedish sleep-tracking company Sleep Cycle compared three years of aggregated, anonymized nighttime-cough data (January 2023 to January 2026) against England's official respiratory surveillance: NHS 111 triage calls, PCR positivity, and hospital admissions. Population-level nighttime coughing rose roughly one week ahead of the corresponding rise in official flu and COVID-19 activity. No individual data left Sleep Cycle's systems; UKHSA ran the comparison against its own indicators. Why it matters: A week of lead time is enough to matter for hospital staffing and vaccine-clinic timing, and the study's design — a national health agency validating a consumer app's aggregate signal against its own gold-standard data, without any individual data changing hands — is a template other health agencies currently lack.

Sources: GOV.UK/UKHSA, The Telegraph

Jonesboro Names Its New AI Dispatcher After the Woman Whose Job It Now Does

The Jonesboro Police Department in Arkansas ran a two-day test of an AI call-taker, Lezle, on its non-emergency line — named for Lezle Wilson, a longtime JPD dispatcher who died in 2023. Lezle takes the caller's name and number, asks questions to determine what's happening, and either logs a non-emergency call for an officer or hands off to a live dispatcher if the situation escalates. Human dispatchers monitor every call in real time and can force an immediate transfer. Why it matters: Non-emergency call volume is the load that burns out small-department dispatch staff without ever making the news when it goes wrong. JPD's design keeps a human monitoring every call rather than auditing after the fact — the detail that decides whether a tool like this is defensible at 911-adjacent scale.

Sources: KATV, KAIT8
