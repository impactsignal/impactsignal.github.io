#168: Impact Signals #168 — 25,000 Small Nonprofits on Claude, Victoria's AI Fire Cameras Watch 1.4 Million Hectares, Ghana Weighs AI Outbreak Forecasts, Microsoft's Diagnosis Panel Heads to a Boston ER

About 25,000 Nonprofits, Most Under 10 Staff, Now Use Claude for Nonprofits; Anthropic Adds a Plugin Built With 30 of Them

The Associated Press reported on Monday, October 26 that Anthropic expanded its nonprofit support on the same day it filed to sell shares to the public. About 25,000 nonprofits use Claude for Nonprofits, and most have fewer than 10 staff members. The new piece is a Claude for Nonprofits plugin that Anthropic built with 30 nonprofits to fit their needs, and the company is widening Claude Corps, which embeds fellows inside nonprofits, by adding consultants for smaller, shorter-term projects. AP did not report counts or dollar figures for the Corps expansion. AP named users: Allies Against Slavery, a Texas anti-trafficking group whose single government affairs staffer used Claude to analyze more than 750 state-level anti-trafficking policies; the Montgomery County Food Bank north of Houston, which uses it for pickup and delivery scheduling and donor contact, with two Claude Corps fellows coming to work on inventory and volunteer scheduling; and the Taproot Foundation, which is working with Anthropic on pro bono expertise for specific projects. Why it matters: The verifiable shift is scale and shape. The user base is small organizations, and the support model is moving from year-long fellowships toward short, project-sized consulting. The named cases are back-office capacity, policy analysis for one staffer and logistics for a food bank, rather than client-facing AI. What comes next is dated: the company's previously reported plan to commit 5 percent of its shares to a public benefit fund still needs shareholder approval after the IPO, which AP says could come as early as November.

Sources: abcnews.com, usnews.com, local10.com

Victoria Grows Its AI Bushfire Camera Network From 7 to 18 Cameras, Watching 1.4 Million Hectares

The Victorian Government announced on Monday, October 26 that it will put $3.8 million over three years into its AI bushfire camera network, jointly funded with Hancock Victorian Plantations and the Green Triangle Fire Alliance. Eleven new AI-powered cameras join the seven already operating, for 18 in total, rolled out across western and eastern Victoria by November 2027. The cameras scan for smoke and fire and alert authorities when conditions need checking. They sit on private plantations but monitor more than 1.4 million hectares, of which 211,000 hectares is plantation, so over 85 percent of the watched area is non-plantation land and nearby regional communities get the early detection too. The existing cameras detected 40 fires in 2023-24 and 46 in 2024-25, per the release, and the expansion follows a joint Commonwealth and Victorian trial of the accuracy of commercial AI bushfire detection technology. Emergency Services Minister Paul Edbrooke: "Every minute counts when a bushfire starts." Why it matters: This is a public-private cost share in which a plantation industry's own asset protection buys smoke detection for the communities around it, with 85 percent of the coverage outside the plantations. It also follows a government accuracy trial rather than a vendor pitch. The next verifiable markers are the camera installs through November 2027 and this summer's detection counts against the 40 and 46 fires of the past two seasons.

Sources: premier.vic.gov.au, themandarin.com.au, councilmagazine.com.au

As Ebola Reaches Nigeria and Kenya, Ghana's Health Service Says It Is Exploring AI to Forecast Outbreaks

Dr Samuel Kaba Akoriyea, Director-General of the Ghana Health Service, said on Joy News' PM Express on Monday, October 26 that the service is exploring artificial intelligence to anticipate disease outbreaks and strengthen emergency planning, MyJoyOnline reported on October 27. The predictive tools under consideration would analyze disease trends, population movement and environmental conditions to flag emerging risks and show where surveillance and resources are needed. Per the report, they would complement existing monitoring, not replace laboratory diagnosis, clinical assessment or established outbreak response. Ghana has detected no Ebola case. It is in a preparedness phase, has run simulation exercises, and monitors developments from its Public Health Emergency Operations Centre at Pantang. The regional pressure is real: in a statement dated October 23, reported by fundsforNGOs News on October 27, CARE said Nigeria confirmed its first Ebola case, in Abuja, Kenya reported nine cases over two weeks, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo outbreak had reached at least 9,704 confirmed cases and more than 4,680 related deaths across 8 of its 26 provinces. Why it matters: This is a stated intention, not a deployed system. No tool, vendor, budget or timeline was named, and the service itself says the value depends on reliable, timely data and the capacity to act on it. What is newly true is that a West African health service facing a cross-border Ebola threat is publicly framing AI forecasting as part of its preparedness, with movement and environmental data as the proposed inputs. The verifiable next step is whether the service names a system or a data partner.

Sources: myjoyonline.com, ghanamma.com, news.fundsforngos.org

Microsoft's AI Diagnosis Panel Lands in Science at 83 Percent on Hard Cases, and a Live Hospital Study in Boston Comes Next

Microsoft's MAI-DxO, a system that coordinates several AI agents to work like a panel of doctors, was published in the journal Science on Thursday, October 22. On complex case records from the New England Journal of Medicine, run as a simulation, it reached a correct diagnosis 83 percent of the time, against 36 percent for primary care doctors and general internists working alone with reference materials, The AI Insider reported. Specialists and physician teams were not part of the comparison. The orchestration helped weaker underlying models the most: Gemini 2.5 Flash rose from 46.4 percent to nearly 69 percent. What is new since last year's preprint is peer review plus a real-world test. Microsoft has partnered with Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston to evaluate the system in emergency and urgent care, comparing its recommendations with those of doctors treating consenting patients. Why it matters: A simulated benchmark on curated case records is not care, and the comparison excluded specialists, so the 83 percent does not establish real-world benefit. The shift is that the claim has cleared peer review in Science and is moving into a prospective study with real patients at a Boston hospital, which is where it will either hold up or not. The finding that orchestration lifts cheaper models most is the detail with reach beyond well-resourced hospitals. No results from the Beth Israel Deaconess study have been reported.

Sources: theaiinsider.tech, techtimes.com
