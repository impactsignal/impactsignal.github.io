#153: Impact Signals #153 — Gates and Wellcome Leap's $100M Stillbirth Bet, Alabama's False-Alarm Scanner Apps, Halton Police AI Hiring

Gates Foundation and Wellcome Leap Commit $100 Million to Halve Stillbirths, With AI Ultrasound Among the Tools

The Gates Foundation and Wellcome Leap announced "Uzima: A Stillbirth Prevention Program" in Berlin on October 12, 2026, with each contributing $50 million over the first three years. Nearly two million babies are stillborn every year, roughly half of them in sub-Saharan Africa, where the program begins. Two of its five funding categories are explicitly AI: decision-support tools that help health workers interpret warning signs, and AI-enabled ultrasound and screening tools for places where specialists and equipment are scarce. Others cover earlier diagnostics for complications such as preeclampsia and gestational diabetes. Why it matters: AI is written into one of the largest single stillbirth commitments as a named category, not a side bet. What is new is the money, the ten-year halving target and an initial package of innovations due by early 2027; what does not yet exist is a named country, a named partner, a breakdown of the $100 million, or evidence that AI-read ultrasound works at this scale. The program says its first participating country will be announced "in the coming months."

Sources: GlobeNewswire (joint release), The Manila Times, dpa via Berliner Abendblatt

Alabama Fire Chiefs Say AI Police-Scanner Apps Are Inventing Emergencies

Elmore-Autauga News reported on October 11, 2026 that central Alabama first responders are fielding false alerts from apps that use AI to transcribe and summarize public-safety radio and push "real-time" warnings to phones. In Prattville, an alert described a serious gas main break; Fire Chief Josh Bingham said contractors had damaged a small pipe inside a home's wall. In Irondale, residents reacted to a report of a firefighter trapped in a burning building that was actually a fire department training exercise. Officials blame noisy audio, overlapping voices and radio jargon read with no human review, and Bingham told residents to delete the apps. Why it matters: The failure mechanism is specific: automated summaries of raw dispatch audio reach the public with no human in the loop, and fire and police departments absorb the cost of answering worried residents and posting corrections. Alabama follows Lamar County, Mississippi (August 14) and Massachusetts police chiefs (August 30), where the CrimeRadar app's own spokesperson said it carried no law-enforcement accuracy reports. No state or platform has yet moved to require labeling or human review, and the Alabama report does not name the apps involved.

Sources: Elmore-Autauga News, WDAM, Athol Daily News

Halton Police Become Canada's First Agency to Use AI to Hire and Train 911 Call Takers

Halton Regional Police, serving the region west of Toronto, announced on October 8, 2026 that it is using a tool from US firm GovWorx to run simulated emergency calls that test applicants' decision-making, communication and crisis management, and to evaluate call takers after hire. Halton says the tool halves candidate evaluation time, its own figure with no independent evaluation cited. The service has 75 dispatchers and call takers, plans to hire four to eight more this year, and says training a new call taker takes close to a year. A separate AI system, SARA, has answered 95,367 non-emergency calls this year and cut almost two seconds off average 911 wait time per call. Why it matters: Most public-safety AI coverage is about what software does on the call; here AI helps decide who sits in the chair, measured against a North American standard of answering 90 percent of 911 calls within 15 seconds. How a simulated call scores a person under stress, and whether that scoring has been validated, is the open question, and Halton has not published that data. The SARA figures are the concrete part: they show how much non-emergency volume an AI front door is already absorbing.

Sources: CP24, CTV News, insauga
