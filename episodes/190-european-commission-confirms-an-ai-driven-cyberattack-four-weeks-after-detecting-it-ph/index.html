#190: Impact Signals #190 — European Commission Confirms an AI-Driven Cyberattack Four Weeks After Detecting It, Philippine School Hazard Platform Reaches 57,000 Users, GoFundMe Notifies Donors After Bot Attacks

Top Stories

The European Commission Confirms an AI-Driven Cyberattack Four Weeks After Detecting It, With Staff Payroll and Medical-Claims Systems Taken Offline

The European Commission, the executive arm of the European Union, confirmed on Thursday, November 19, that it was the target of a cyberattack carried out with artificial intelligence tools. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier told reporters the attack was detected on October 22 and confirmed on October 26 after an initial investigation, Brussels Signal and Cyprus Inform both reported. It became public only on Thursday morning, when Politico reported that internal services had been disrupted for more than 10 days. "This was an AI-generated cybercampaign," Regnier said, adding that the attackers had misused an AI tool available to the public and that he was not aware of any ransom demand. The Associated Press, as cited by Brussels Signal, reported that it was the first time the European Union had disclosed a cyberattack using AI. The systems named are the ones staff use for their own affairs: Sysper, the human resources platform for salaries, leave and timesheets, and MyPMO, which handles medical expense claims. According to Brussels Signal, the platforms store names, pay grades, home addresses and contact details, and are separate from the system that hosts the Commission's internal documents. The Commission took several applications offline as a precaution and says most have been restored; Sysper was working again on Thursday while the staff health insurance scheme remained unavailable, two EU officials told Euronews, as cited by Brussels Signal. Escudo Digital, summarizing Politico, adds that salaries were paid as usual and that Commissioner Henna Virkkunen briefed the weekly meeting of commissioners. Why it matters: A major public institution has now said on the record that a publicly available AI tool was used against it, and the damage landed on the ordinary administrative systems that hold staff pay, addresses and medical claims. The dates are the second finding: detected October 22, confirmed internally October 26, acknowledged November 19 after a news report. Officials told Euronews they had received no formal internal communication, and according to Politico, technical support at first blamed the Sysper outages on a general malfunction. Dutch member of the European Parliament Bart Groothuis told Euronews his problem with the Commission was that "they keep the information about it internally." The limits are firm. Regnier declined to say whether the attack was over, how large it was, who was behind it, which tool was used or whether the Commission used AI in its response, and he said investigators had so far found no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole. Brussels Signal counts this as the third known cyberattack on Commission systems this year, after a January 30 intrusion into the system that manages staff mobile devices and a March break-in in which CERT-EU, the cybersecurity service for EU institutions, says about 91.7 gigabytes of compressed data were taken. CERT-EU and the European Data Protection Supervisor have been informed and the investigation is open; its outcome will settle whether personal data left the Commission's systems.

Sources: Brussels Signal, Cyprus Inform, Escudo Digital

The Philippine Department of Education Reports 57,000 Users of Its School Hazard Platform Three Months After Launch and Plans a Dropout Warning System for 300 Schools in 2027

The Philippine Department of Education (DepEd) on Thursday, November 19, marked the second anniversary of its Education Center for AI Research (ECAIR) in Makati City by reporting usage numbers for tools already in schools and expansion plans for 2027, the Daily Tribune and Newsbytes.PH reported the same day. Its school safety platform, LIGTAS+ (Learning Institution Geohazard Tracking and Assessment for Safety), has reached more than 57,000 users since its launch in August, most of them DepEd personnel according to Newsbytes.PH. The platform draws on the national weather agency PAGASA, the national volcanology and seismology institute PHIVOLCS and global AI weather models to deliver hazard alerts specific to a school site, and school disaster risk reduction teams have used it to generate more than 6,000 situation reports. The second tool is a dropout early warning system called Talaan, short for Teachers' and Administrators' Learner-centric Almanac and Analytics Nest. It analyzes student data to flag learners at risk of dropping out, was designed with about 120 teachers and school heads from three schools in Las Piñas City, and will be expanded to 300 schools next year. Newsbytes.PH adds that ECAIR trained 52 personnel from DepEd and the national technical education agency to build their own AI tools. The department also confirmed a nationwide 2027 rollout of the Grade 2 screening tool this show covered on November 18. Why it matters: A national school system is publishing usage figures for AI tools after launch, beyond announcing them: 57,000 users and 6,000 situation reports in roughly three months for a platform that tells a school what hazard is coming to its own site. The dropout system is at an earlier stage, and the reports show the distance: design input from three schools and a target of 300. Neither report says how many schools use Talaan today, what data it reads, how accurate its flags are, or what happens for a learner once flagged. All the figures are the ministry's own, carried by two outlets. Education Secretary Sonny Angara said the expansion would focus on maintaining tools already in use and on public trust: "technology must empower people, and not replace them." Majah-Leah Ravago, director of the partner center SEAMEO INNOTECH, put the sequence as: "First, sustain what has worked. Second, scale what is ready." The 2027 expansion is where the accuracy of the flags and the follow-up for flagged learners will first be visible at scale.

Sources: Daily Tribune, Newsbytes.PH

GoFundMe Confirms the Bot Attacks on Nonprofit Fundraising Platforms Reached Personal Donor Information and Begins Notifying People, While DonorPerfect Says It Cannot Yet Say What Was Taken

This is a new development in a story this show reported on November 13, when AI bots hit several nonprofit fundraising platforms at once and the companies described their steps as precautions. The NonProfit Times reported on Thursday, November 19, that a GoFundMe spokesperson told the paper: "Our investigation found that an unauthorized third party has made repeated attempts to access GoFundMe Pro and was able to gain access to personal donor and supporter information." GoFundMe said it began emailing the people involved on November 19, is mailing notices to people in North Dakota and Washington and notifying regulators in those two states, and has opened a call center. Its notices describe a security incident involving GoFundMe Pro and do not name any nonprofit. Salesforce, whose link to GoFundMe Pro was cut last week, told The NonProfit Times it disabled the connection "after identifying suspicious use of access tokens associated with the third-party provider," and that the problem did not come from a flaw in its own platform. The Chronicle of Philanthropy reported on November 17 that GoFundMe Pro told customers the attacker obtained credentials tied to that connection and may have used them to reach those organizations' own Salesforce records. According to The NonProfit Times, organizations using the connection have been told to replace stored keys and passwords and to review account activity going back to October 14. At DonorPerfect, which serves more than 11,000 customers according to the Chronicle, the answer on data is still open. Lauren Sheehan, president of parent company SofterWare, told The NonProfit Times the bots came in through the company's marketing website and that "we do not have a determination of who was impacted or what if anything was taken." The company took customer access down on the evening of November 10, and the Chronicle says the FBI is investigating. Why it matters: The question nonprofits were asking on November 13 now has an answer at one company: donor information was reached at GoFundMe Pro, and formal notification has started, including to two state regulators. The reach may extend past the fundraising platform, because the credentials taken opened a door into some organizations' own donor databases. It arrives during year-end fundraising; the Chronicle describes nonprofits unable to reach their donor data while running campaigns and donor meetings. The two outlets differ on recovery: the Chronicle quotes Sheehan saying 99 percent of systems were restored by the afternoon of November 16, while The NonProfit Times wrote on November 19 that the DonorPerfect application remained temporarily unavailable. A SofterWare official, speaking on background, said "an AI agent was rapidly adapting its approaches and discovering new methods to attack systems within seconds or minutes"; that description comes from the affected company and has not been independently confirmed. Sheehan told the Chronicle that two other platform chief executives told her privately they were also attacked, and none has said so publicly. Still unknown, by the companies' own account: how many donors and which organizations are involved, whether anything was taken from DonorPerfect, and who is behind it. The investigations at both companies and by the FBI remain open.

Sources: The NonProfit Times, The Chronicle of Philanthropy
