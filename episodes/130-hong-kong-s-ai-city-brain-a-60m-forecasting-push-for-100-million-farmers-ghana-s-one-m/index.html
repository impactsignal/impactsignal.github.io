Hong Kong Puts Disaster Response Under One "AI City Brain" With a Chief Secretary Task Force and a New AI Commissioner

In his Policy Address to the Legislative Council on September 16, 2026, Chief Executive John Lee said Hong Kong will build an "AI City Brain", an integrated platform for disaster prevention, emergency response and urban resilience drawing on AI, big data and Internet-of-Things sensing. A task force led by the Chief Secretary for Administration will sit above the Security Bureau, the Transport and Logistics Bureau, the Development Bureau and the Innovation, Technology and Industry Bureau; phase one integrates the digital infrastructure of the Security Bureau's Emergency Monitoring and Support Centre. Alongside it, the Transport Department will study an AI traffic management platform covering extreme weather and major events, and the police will build an Inter-departmental Major Incident Platform to centralise real-time data during an incident. The same address announced an AI commissioner expected to start next year and said the government will consider deepfake legislation. The work sits inside Hong Kong's first Five-Year Plan for Economic and Social Development, covering 2026 to 2030. Why it matters: Emergency coordination in Hong Kong has run through separate bureau systems, which is why phase one is described as an integration rather than a build, and naming the Chief Secretary as chair puts the platform above any single bureau's budget line. The AI commissioner post creates a named official who can be asked, next typhoon season, what the platform saw and when. Verifiably next: the commissioner's appointment and the Emergency Monitoring and Support Centre integration.

Sources: Hong Kong Free Press, South China Morning Post

A $10 Million Grant to Notre Dame's AIM for Scale Inside a $60 Million OpenAI Foundation Package Aimed at 100 Million Smallholder Farmers

The Agricultural Innovation Mechanism for Scale (AIM for Scale), housed at the University of Notre Dame's Keough School of Global Affairs, announced on September 10, 2026 that it has received $10 million, one piece of a $60 million OpenAI Foundation investment that also funds the University of Chicago, UC Berkeley, Precision Development, Digital Green and CIMMYT. The stated goal across those partners is AI-powered weather and crop-disease forecasting for 100 million smallholder farmers over three years, concentrated in South and Southeast Asia and East Africa. AIM for Scale's own role is the unglamorous half, helping governments move these services from pilot to sustained national delivery; CIMMYT's piece is wheat pathogen forecasting built on AI plus NASA Earth-observation data. Delivery to farmers runs through text messages, calls and chatbots rather than a new app. Paul Winters, speaking for AIM for Scale, framed the problem as distribution rather than invention: "Artificial intelligence is creating extraordinary new possibilities for farmers, but innovation alone is not enough." Why it matters: AI weather models produce localised forecasts on substantially less compute than traditional numerical forecasting, which is the technical reason a national meteorological service in a low-income country can now run one. The money is aimed at the government hand-off, the step where most agricultural advisory pilots die. Verifiably next: whether any named partner country moves a forecasting service onto its own ministry budget inside the three-year window.

Sources: University of Notre Dame

UNESCO Signs Ghana's TVET Service and Education Trust Fund Onto a One-Million-Learner AI Training Target

UNESCO published on September 11, 2026 that it has formalised partnerships with the Ghana TVET Service and the Ghana Education Trust Fund (GETFund) to scale AI EmpowerED, a teacher-training programme inside UNESCO's Global Skills Academy supported by Microsoft Elevate, KPMG International and Tablet Academy. The national target is up to one million technical and vocational educators and learners trained and certified by the end of 2027. The pilot, which began in 2025 at AAMUSTED University, produced more than 30 master trainers and roughly 3,300 learners holding Microsoft certifications in AI and digital skills. Phase two extends to about 140 TVET institutions through four consecutive three-month regional cycles on a train-the-trainer model. Paul Adjei of GETFund and Eric Kofi Adzroe, director-general of the Ghana TVET Service, signed for the Ghanaian side; Ghana is one of six countries in the initiative, alongside India, Kenya, Malaysia, Uganda and Tanzania. Why it matters: The gap between 3,300 learners certified in the pilot and one million by end-2027 is a factor of about 300, and the mechanism named for closing it is 140 institutions running four cycles rather than additional central funding. Tying in GETFund matters because that is the domestic education financing vehicle, not a donor line item. Verifiably next: the first regional cycle's completion count against the 140-institution roster.

Sources: UNESCO

Pakistan's National Telco Puts an AI Sign-Language Agent on the Customer-Service Counter in Three Cities

Pakistan Telecommunication Company Limited (PTCL) is installing SILA, an AI sign-language agent built by the Pakistani accessibility company ConnectHear, across its customer-care centres, starting with Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad. Reported September 15, 2026 by PhoneWorld and September 16 by ProPakistani and TechJuice, SILA lets a Deaf customer sign their query and deal directly with a customer-service representative, with no interpreter booked in advance and no third party in the conversation. Amad Khan, PTCL's chief marketing officer, framed it as connectivity being "meaningful only when it enables everyone to participate"; Azima Dhanjee is ConnectHear's chief executive and co-founder. The two organisations previously built SUNO, an AI-driven early-warning service that pushed emergency alerts in Pakistani Sign Language over WhatsApp during natural and man-made disasters. Why it matters: Interpreter scarcity is the usual reason a Deaf customer cannot complete a routine transaction without bringing a hearing relative, which is a privacy cost as much as a time cost, and putting the agent at the counter rather than in an app the customer must find moves the burden onto the service provider. The same pairing already shipped disaster alerts in Pakistani Sign Language. Verifiably next: whether SILA extends past the three launch cities, and whether the alert channel and the service counter share one sign-language model.

Sources: ProPakistani, PhoneWorld

Liverpool City Region Names a 15-Member AI Task Force Under the UK's First Regional Chief AI Officer

Liverpool City Region Mayor Steve Rotheram named the 15 members of a new AI Task Force on September 16, 2026, chaired by Nicola Hodson, former chief executive and chair of IBM UK and Ireland. Members include Kate Warriner of Alder Hey Children's NHS Foundation Trust, Katherine Royse of the STFC Hartree Centre and Gary McAllister of Dell Technologies. The task force advises Tiffany St James, appointed as the United Kingdom's first regional chief AI officer, on a roadmap covering inclusive growth, public services and outcomes for the region's 1.6 million residents. Rotheram's stated ambition is for the city region to be "the home of 'AI for Good'". No budget figure and no roadmap deadline were published with the announcement. Why it matters: A regional chief AI officer with a named advisory board is an accountable structure rather than a strategy document, and the membership says where it points: a children's hospital trust and a national supercomputing centre sit alongside a hardware vendor. The absent numbers are the tell. Verifiably next: the roadmap itself, and whether it carries a budget and named public services or stays at the level of ambition.

Sources: LocalGov, Liverpool City Region Combined Authority
